# Frameworks — Tocqueville's named constructs

Load when a construct's exact sense matters. Preserve his terms; a paraphrase is not interchangeable.

- **Equality of conditions (l'égalité des conditions)** — the master social fact of the modern age:
  the levelling of ranks and the fluidity of property and status. For Tocqueville it is *providential*
  and irresistible, more *just* than aristocracy, yet not simply *good* — the generative fact from
  which laws, opinions, and passions derive. (DiA, both vols; the pivot of the whole work.)

- **Point of departure (point de départ)** — the founding condition that contains a society's whole
  future as a seed contains the plant; his term, fixed on the Puritan township. To understand or
  forecast a nation, return to its origin. (DiA v1 ch.2; method of *Old Regime*.)

- **Mœurs** — untranslatable; the whole moral and intellectual condition of a people: habits, beliefs,
  opinions, the "habits of the heart." Ranked *above* laws and physical circumstances as the cause of
  political outcomes. Translation always interprets — keep the French in view. (DiA v1 ch.17.)

- **Individualism (individualisme)** — his coinage, pejorative: not selfishness but a considered,
  democratic withdrawal into one's small circle that saps public life. The disease for which civil
  association is the cure. (DiA v2 bk.2.)

- **Tutelary / soft despotism** — the novel democratic danger: a mild, "absolute, minute, regular,
  provident" central power that provides for and manages an equal, isolated people, keeping them in
  perpetual childhood. Not tyranny but infantilization; compatible with democratic forms. (DiA v2 bk.4
  ch.6.)

- **Tyranny of the majority** — the majority's combined legislative, executive, moral, and electoral
  omnipotence, with no counterweight; coercion migrating from the body to opinion. Note: prominent in
  1835 (v1); the phrase itself recedes by 1840, the concern migrating into soft despotism. (DiA v1
  ch.15–16.)

- **Self-interest rightly understood (intérêt bien entendu)** — the American moral doctrine that
  private interest, enlightened and long-sighted, leads men to serve the public. Tocqueville finds it
  low but sturdy — not noble, but a workable foundation for a democratic ethic. (DiA v2 bk.2.)

- **Art of associating** — the mother-science of a free democracy: the learned habit of forming
  voluntary bodies that do the resistance-work aristocracy once got from permanent great men. (DiA v2.)

- **Providential fact** — his frame for equality (and, dangerously, for Native extinction): a
  historical movement so large it looks like destiny. He uses it to convey scale — and, in the colonial
  case, it risks laundering human choice into fate (see convictions-and-contradictions.md, J13).

- **Domination vs. colonization** — his operative distinction in the Algeria writings: *domination* =
  military mastery of the Arab population; *colonization* = European settlement. He pursues both and
  subordinates the first to the second. (Algeria Reports, 1847.)
